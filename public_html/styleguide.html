Style guide

How odhranroche.com looks, sounds and behaves. Read this before building a new page. Everything shown here uses the live stylesheet, assets/css/site.css, so it can't drift from the site.

Principles Colour Type Space and layout Shape and depth Components Illustration Motion Voice and copy Accessibility What to avoid Building a new page

Principles

  • Written for a quick readThe main reader is a recruiter or hiring manager with two minutes. Lead with what I do, then who I did it for. Leave detail and dates to the CV.
  • One bold thing per pageEach page gets one memorable element, like the garden on the homepage. Everything around it stays quiet: hairlines, plain text, white space.
  • The garden is the systemSoil is documentation, water is training, green is growth. Colour, illustration and motion all follow that idea, so it has to mean something wherever it appears.
  • Readable firstBoth typefaces were designed for legibility. Text stays dark, short and left-aligned. Nothing important depends on hover or animation.
  • Human marks, used sparinglyThe handwritten notes and the sticker show a person built this. Use at most one per screen, and always point it at something real.

Colour

Every colour family has one job. Use tan and brown only when something is about documentation, and blue only when it's about training. Green marks growth, links and anything you can interact with. Contrast figures are against the page background.

Rules

  • Body text is --ink. Secondary text is --ink-soft. Never go lighter than that for text.
  • --leaf and --sky are under 4.5:1, so they're for lines, icons and illustration only. For green or blue text, use --leaf-deep or --sky-deep.
  • Hover backgrounds on quiet controls are always --leaf-tint. Green means "you can act on this".
  • The site is light only for now. Write colours as tokens so a dark theme can be added later by redefining them.
  • Don't add new colours. If something needs emphasis, use weight or size first.

Type

Lexend

--f-display. Headings, navigation, buttons, labels. Weights 400, 500 and 600. It was designed to improve reading fluency, which suits a site about learning.

Atkinson Hyperlegible

--f-body. All running text. Regular and bold. Made by the Braille Institute so similar letters (I, l, 1) are easy to tell apart.

Kalam

--f-hand. Handwritten notes and the sticker only. Never for headings, buttons or more than a few words.

Rules

  • Use sentence case for every heading, button and label: "Building skills", not "Building Skills" or "BUILDING SKILLS".
  • Don't use uppercase labels or small "eyebrow" text above headings.
  • Use one h1 per page. --t-display is only for the name on the homepage. Inner pages use --t-title.
  • Headings use negative letter-spacing (-0.02em to -0.035em) and text-wrap: balance.
  • Left-align text. Only centre a caption that sits directly under a centred illustration.
  • Use bold for emphasis. Don't italicise for emphasis, and don't underline anything that isn't a link.

  • Everything lines up on the left edge of .wrap. Don't centre whole sections.
  • Separate sections with a hairline (1px solid var(--rule)) or by switching to .section-alt, never both on the same edge.
  • Split layouts are two columns at most and stack into one below 860px.
  • Use gap on flex and grid parents instead of margins on children.
  • Ledes under page and section headings run the full content width. Don't cap them at half the page.

Shape and depth

  • There are two corner radii: --r-md (10px) for buttons, nav items and tiles, and --r-sm (6px) for small controls and the sticker.
  • Only two things get a border: logo tiles and screenshots, because both need a plain edge against the page. Don't put content in cards.
  • Tinted areas (skill map plots and panels) use a topic tint with no border and no shadow. Use at most one panel per section.
  • Only the sticker, the Experience menu (which floats over the page) and the homepage garden have shadows. Use hairlines and space to group things, not shadows.
  • Don't use gradient fills on anything except the faint .section-alt background and the shading inside scene illustrations.

Illustration

All illustrations are inline SVG drawn in the garden language, with no outlines and round line caps. The colours come from the palette above. There are two levels of finish:

  • Icons (the small pictures below, skill map plots, sprouts in lists) are flat: one colour per shape, no gradients or texture.
  • Scene illustrations (the homepage garden) are richer: soft light from the top left, made with gradients that stay inside one colour family; a fine grain on soil; slightly hand-drawn edges; and faint soft shadows. Keep it subtle: no glows, no shine on anything but water and the can, and no gradient that crosses colour families.
  • Soil and seeds mean documentation, water means training, and plants mean results. Don't use these images for anything else.
  • The homepage garden is one scene, not a chart: a cross-section of a garden bed whose soil layers are drawn as lines of writing, a watering can, and the plant they feed. It uses the scene finish described above. Hovering or focusing the soil makes the writing legible, grows the roots and sprouts the seeds; the can tips and pours. Either one lengthens the "Reliable growth" underline.
  • Use angular or furrowed shapes for soil. A single rounded brown mound reads as something else.
  • Leaves use the shared #leaf path in three greens (in the garden scene, as green-to-green gradients with a pale midrib). Vary their rotation and scale, never their shape.
  • Something clickable inside an illustration gets a hand-drawn underline under its label, a pointer cursor, a hover response and a visible focus outline.
  • Don't use photos, stock illustrations, emoji or icon sets to decorate. Use a small inline SVG icon only where it helps someone understand or act, like the download arrow.
  • Company and brand logos are always the official files, never redrawn.

  • Motion responds to something the visitor did, like a hover, focus or tap. The one exception is the homepage plant and its roots growing once when the page loads.
  • Don't reveal content as you scroll or use parallax. Everything is visible when the page loads.
  • Nothing moves more than about 8px or rotates more than about 15°.
  • With reduced motion turned on, all animation stops. The site already handles this.

Voice and copy

  • Write in the first person, plainly, the way I'd say it in an interview. Use short sentences in the active voice.
  • Say what I did and for whom, with concrete nouns: "onboarding programmes", "service guides". Leave out adjectives like "passionate", "innovative" or "seamless".
  • On overview pages, don't show dates, cities or grades. Those go on the CV.
  • Don't put reference quotes on the homepage. If they're used anywhere, they go on their own page with the person's name and role.
  • Buttons and links say exactly what happens: "Download CV", "Get in touch", "Read my CV". Not "Learn more" or "Click here".
  • Spelling is British/Irish English: "-ise" endings (organisation, standardised, specialising), "programme" for training programmes ("program" only for computer software), and "practise" as a verb but "practice" as a noun. Quotes, course names and text transcribed from images keep their original spelling.
  • Write job titles in title case because they're names ("Senior Technical Writer"). Everything else is in sentence case.

Accessibility

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA on every page. Check these before you upload:

  • Text contrast is at least 4.5:1. The tokens above already pass if you use them as documented.
  • Every interactive element can be reached with Tab and shows the blue focus outline.
  • Touch targets are at least 44px tall (--tap).
  • Each page has a skip link, one main, one h1, and headings in order.
  • Decorative SVGs have aria-hidden="true". Meaningful ones have a title or aria-label.
  • Links that open a new tab, or download a file, say so in their accessible name.
  • Images have alt text that describes what they show. The image viewer returns focus to the image when it closes.
  • Pages print cleanly without the header and footer. Check the CV in print preview after changes.
  • Nothing depends on hover alone. Hover text on the homepage is extra, and the links still work by tapping.

What to avoid

Two earlier designs were rejected, one for looking AI-generated and one for being too plain. These are the patterns that caused the first problem.

Also avoid:

  • Grids of cards, each with an icon in a rounded square.
  • Stat counters ("12+ years", "500 learners trained") and progress bars for skills.
  • Purple or blue gradients, glows, glass effects, gradient text.
  • Emoji as icons or section markers.
  • Numbered section markers (01, 02, 03) when the order means nothing.
  • Pills and tags for every skill.
  • Centring every section.
  • Testimonials, "Trusted by" logo strips, and calls to action at the bottom of pages. The header already has the navigation, so pages just end.
  • Stock photos and generic illustrations of people at laptops.

Building a new page

  1. Copy page-template.html (it has the full header and footer) or the skeleton below into a new file in public_html/, and remove the noindex line.
  2. Set the title, description, canonical URL and og: lines, and add aria-current="page" to the page's own nav link. Add the page to sitemap.xml.
  3. Write the page head: one h1 and a one- or two-sentence lede.
  4. Add sections. Each one is a .section with a .wrap and a .section-head. Alternate with .section-alt if the page is long.
  5. Choose the one bold element for the page. Keep everything else quiet.
  6. Run through the accessibility list above, check the page at 400px wide and in print preview, then upload.
